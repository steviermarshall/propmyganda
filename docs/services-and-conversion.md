# Add-on services, prices, and how /start grades against the best checkouts

Research date: 9 November 2026. Prices are US market, NYC where it mattered, each tied to a source the research pulled. Anything that could not be tied to a published price is flagged.

## Part 1 · What artists buy and what it costs now

PMG's anchors: Basic $550 (30-minute shoot, collab post, small ad spend, report), Mid $1,000, Viral $15,000. The gap between $1,000 and $15,000 is wide open; the market fills it with $2,500 to $5,000 release bundles.

### Markup and bundling

The classic agency model is a 15% media commission, which equals a 17.65% markup on net cost, and production invoices are commonly marked up 15 to 25%. Music agencies now advertise "ad spend never marked up" and charge a management fee instead. Bundling is standard at the top of the market: Davy sells a single release campaign from $5,000 for 30 days, EP $9,500, album $18,000, with paid social, PR and pre-save layered on. The bundle band observed is $2,500 to $5,000.

### What PMG should charge

## Part 2 · How /start grades against the best-converting flows

### The benchmarks

| Metric | Figure | Source, year |
|---|---|---|
| Average cart abandonment | 70% (mean of 50 studies) | Baymard, updated Oct 2026 |
| Top fixable reasons | Extra costs 40%, no trust with card 19%, forced account 18%, too long 17%, site errors 17%, total not visible 12%, too few payment methods 9% | Baymard 2026 |
| Lift available from checkout UX fixes | 35% average | Baymard 2026 |

What Apple does, per Baymard and Apple's own guidance: guest checkout first, account only after purchase, Apple Pay above the fold with no interim screens, pull name and email from the wallet instead of asking, estimated dates not speeds, order tracking by order number plus email. Where Apple fails Baymard's tests: a required phone field with no explanation, and split first/last name fields. Microsoft Store has no public teardown; Baymard's detail is paywalled.

Ten passes, five partials, nine fails. Against the 70% abandonment baseline and Baymard's 35% average lift from fixing checkout UX, the structure is right and the finish is not.

### The grade

**Structure: A-.** Five short steps, no account, prices on the first screen, four required fields on the paid path, live calendar, skippable upload, resume by link. That is what Apple does, and what your UX designer set out to do. Nothing about the shape of the flow needs to change.

**Finish: C.** The things Apple and Stripe sweat are missing: per-field validation, required and optional marks, autofill, pay-later on the two plans most people will buy, reminders, proof, help on the page, and any way to measure where people drop. These are small fixes with the big numbers attached to them.

**Measurement: F.** There is no analytics and no funnel view. Orders are created at the Details step, so the database already knows who started and who paid, but nobody can see the drop-off. Until this exists, every change is a guess.
